# Audit of Poissonized higher coverage and indicator switching

November 27, 2026. This is an internal proof audit of the [higher-coverage manuscript](../papers/higher_coverage.tex) and the [pair-switch manuscript](../papers/pair_indicator_coupling.tex). Both assume independent Poisson clocks with finite total intensity and nonnegative weights with total at most $W$ per positive-rate batch. Write $V>0$ for the variance of the weighted count. The fixed-sample problem has separate hypotheses and a separate audit.

## Higher coverage under arbitrary overlap

- **Threshold two.** For a finite set of deficient species, an internal pair clock can ring only once and its endpoints then have no remaining capacity. Such internal edges form a matching. Each unmatched vertex contributes one plus the sum of the boundary intensities. The common exponential factor includes each clock meeting the set once.
- **Actual variance.** The common/private Poisson interpolation gives both the mean Stein mass $V$ and the energy bound. Revealing whole clocks splits the variance calculation into two incident groups and their intersection. In the far group, remove all clocks meeting either of the two comparison species. The independent residual mean is at least half the original mean, including when the comparison species coincide. Expanding the square before summing retains the cross terms required for the energy bound.
- **Constant and countable limit.** The four contributions to the standard deviation have coefficients $2\sqrt{\ell_r},\sqrt{2b_r},\sqrt{\ell_r},\sqrt{\ell_r}$, where $b_r=rp_r(r)\le\ell_r$. The forward-displacement estimate gives $12+2\sqrt2<15$ when $W\le\sqrt V$. Finite projections preserve marginal and common-clock means. Their nonnegative Stein masses increase to the full mass and are bounded by $W^2\sum_C a_C$.
- **Complex disk.** Conditional clock ratios are estimated with the target factor removed, so each reinsertion uses the lower-order induction under both pinnings. The squared conditional density costs at most $1+196(|z|W)^2(1-e^{-a_C})$. For a free incident mean exceeding $2r$ the Poisson lower tail absorbs the product cost. The real-MGF bound also bounds the real part of the analytic log MGF by the triangle inequality. This proves the disk of radius $1/(64W\sqrt r)$ and the stated cumulant estimates.
- **Power obstruction.** For the star of unit-rate edges, Gaussian tilting of the limiting leaf count shifts the standardized hub total by $e^{-1}t$. Differentiating the hub probability gives $\kappa_4\sim-4e^{-3}\phi(0)n^{3/2}$. The limits for all fixed even orders force exponent at least $1/2$ in a simultaneous cumulant scale $Wr^\alpha$. A bounded hub still gives a Kolmogorov error of order at most $n^{-1/2}$.

## Switching on pair graphs

- **Marginals and switch cost.** The two environments share their incident marks and nonincident clocks. Conditioning the two independent Poisson totals to lie on opposite sides of the chosen threshold gives the required conditional marginals. In the unconditioned comparison, the two evaluations given the marks are iid. First-order projections cost at most $W$ times their squared norms. Each second-order projection indexed by an unordered pair of neighbors occurs twice in the ordered triangle sum. Orthogonality gives the cost bounds $3WV$ and, on triangle-free graphs, $2WV$.
- **Third cumulant.** Equal and repeated indices contribute at most $Wv_0+3W(V-v_0)/2$, where $v_0$ is the sum of individual weighted indicator variances. The distinct-vertex expansion has a path term and a triangle term. Counting the three possible path centers and the ordered triangles against the same projection budget gives at most $3Wv_0$. Association gives $v_0\le V$.
- **Independent components.** After deleting weight at most $H$ per component, the surviving indicators there must be independent. Their centered sum and the bounded deleted contribution are allowed to be dependent. The proof uses the one-component Gaussian estimate and fourth moments in the Miroshnikov–Rogozin concentration inequality, with the scale equal to $\max(W,H)$. In the diffuse case it applies this estimate to the independent *outside* sum before conditioning on a component. For a dominant component its matching Gaussian smooths the replacements of the other components. A center of each star supplies $H\le W$. See [Bobkov and Chistyakov, equations (1.2)–(1.3)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10959-013-0504-1).
- **Unrestricted pair graphs.** The uniform point in a length-weighted switch interval has the translated zero-bias law. Comparing its distribution function to the anchored endpoint gives $d_K\le 2(\|B\|_\infty+\|J\|_\infty)/V$, with $\int J\le3WV$. The mean-square switch estimate controls this integral. Uniform cutoff estimates of order $W\sqrt V$ for the bias and crossing functions remain open. A rare star configuration excludes an absolute conditional-mass bound for this switch, even on examples covered by the star-forest theorem.

## Corrections and reproducibility

The pair verifier formerly computed its CDF upper bound after dropping the upper Poisson tail. If $m$ is an upper bound on the omitted unnormalized length-weighted mass, both the anchor and crossing terms can change by as much as $m$. The reported bound now adds $2m$ inside their sum before multiplying by $2/V$. The program also compares the uniform-point CDF against the independently computed stop-loss identity

$$
F_*(t)=-\mathbb E[(Y-\mathbb EY)(t-Y)_+]/V.
$$

A deliberately short cutoff gives $m\approx0.02225$, exposing a visible CDF difference of about $0.03993$ within the tail allowance. Two species with a common threshold of 24 and only one shared clock give an independent closed-form check of the displacement costs. Numerical comparisons allow for floating-point rounding.

The higher-coverage suite checks all 16 subset coefficients on a four-species threshold-two graph, 24 conditional Mehler evaluations, two Stein-mass models, 13 complex pinning prefixes, and finite star cumulants. The pair suite checks path and triangle switches, the cubic expansion, heterogeneous forests through 60 components, and a conditional-mass lower bound. Run `make check-higher-coverage check-pair-coupling` for the two new suites or `make all` for all seven suites and six PDF validations. [Higher-coverage report](../reports/higher_coverage_verification.json) · [Pair-switch report](../reports/pair_coupling_verification.json).

The finite checks exercise the formulas and their implementations. The uniform constants, countable limits and asymptotic obstructions depend on the manuscript proofs. The threshold-uniform Berry–Esseen conjecture on unrestricted pair graphs remains open after this audit.
